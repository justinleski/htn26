## AI Marketing Copilot for Shopify Merchants

A browser-based marketing platform for Shopify merchants that analyzes their store, ads, customer feedback, and performance data, then automatically helps them improve and create new marketing campaigns.

Instead of a merchant manually checking Shopify, Facebook Ads, Google Ads, reviews, and website analytics separately, our tool brings the information together and answers:

* What are my best-performing ads?
* Why are they performing well?
* What product features do customers care about most?
* What captions/hooks should I test next?
* What type of campaign should I run next?
* Which messaging is actually converting customers?

### Example

A merchant sells rain jackets.

The system analyzes:

* Shopify product and sales data
* customer reviews
* ad performance
* click-through rates
* conversions
* A/B test results
* which products/pages customers interact with most

It discovers:

> Customers consistently mention waterproofing in positive reviews, and waterproof-focused ads have the highest CTR and conversion rate.

The tool then recommends:

> “Waterproofing appears to be your strongest selling point. Ads focused on waterproof protection convert better than your other messaging.”

It can then generate:

* new ad captions
* hooks
* hashtags
* campaign concepts
* A/B test variations
* UGC/content-creator briefs
* short video scripts
* landing-page copy
* email/social copy

For the hackathon, we do **not** need to generate full finished videos. The product can analyze existing marketing and generate the strategy, copy, campaign ideas, and creator briefs.

---

## Core Product Flow

**Store + ads + customer feedback → understand what works → recommend strategy → generate campaign → test variations**

Example:

1. Merchant connects/imports their Shopify store.
2. We ingest product, sales, and review information.
3. Merchant imports or connects advertising-performance data.
4. System analyzes patterns across CTR, conversions, reviews, and products.
5. Dashboard surfaces the strongest findings.
6. Merchant clicks **Generate Campaign**.
7. System creates a campaign based on actual performance insights.
8. Multiple caption/hook variations are generated for future A/B testing.

# 3. Elastic / Elasticsearch — Marketing Data Search + Insights

### Sponsor tool

* Elasticsearch

### How we use it

Elastic could become the system's **context/search layer**.

We may have:

* thousands of customer reviews
* product descriptions
* historical ads
* captions
* campaign metrics
* customer feedback
* scraped marketing content

Instead of dumping all of that into an LLM, Elasticsearch can retrieve the most relevant information.

Example query:

> “What do customers value most about this jacket?”

Elastic searches:

* reviews
* previous ad copy
* product information
* campaign results

Then the AI receives relevant evidence.

Example insight:

> 38% of high-rated reviews mention waterproofing, while only 7% mention style.

Elastic specifically wants projects that turn messy, unstructured data into actionable information, including agentic systems using hybrid search and retrieval.

### Why this is interesting technically

It makes the system more than:

> send everything to ChatGPT.

Architecture becomes:

**messy marketing data → search/retrieval → relevant evidence → AI reasoning → action**

**Priority: VERY GOOD if backend has time**

# 4. Huawei Multi-Agent — Virtual Marketing Team

### Sponsor tool

* JiuwenSwarm / WorkSwarm

Huawei wants multiple specialized agents genuinely collaborating rather than simply chaining prompts.

### How we use it

Instead of one model doing everything:

**Marketing Director Agent**

* coordinates overall campaign

**Analytics Agent**

* analyzes CTR/conversion data

**Customer Insight Agent**

* analyzes reviews and feedback

**Creative Agent**

* generates campaign concepts

**Copywriter Agent**

* generates hooks/captions

**Brand Agent**

* checks whether generated content matches the store's voice

**Critic Agent**

* verifies claims against product data

Example:

Analytics Agent:

> Waterproof-focused ads have the highest conversion rate.

Customer Insight Agent:

> Waterproofing is also frequently praised in reviews.

Creative Agent:

> Let's build the next campaign around commuting in bad weather.

Brand Agent:

> Avoid overly technical language because this merchant's existing brand tone is casual.

Then the Campaign Director combines everything.

### Why it fits

This is genuine task decomposition and agent collaboration around a real-world business problem, which is exactly what Huawei's multi-agent challenge asks for.

**Priority: STRONG if backend wants something technically ambitious**

# 7. Rox — Marketing Agent Working With Messy Data

### How it fits

Rox specifically wants agents that operate on messy, incomplete, conflicting real-world information and then take meaningful action.

Marketing data is exactly like that.

Imagine:

Facebook Ads:

> Campaign A has highest CTR.

Shopify:

> Campaign B resulted in more purchases.

Reviews:

> Customers love waterproofing.

Website analytics:

> People spend the most time looking at insulation information.

The system needs to reason:

> High clicks do not necessarily mean high conversions.

Then conclude:

> Campaign B is actually stronger from a revenue perspective, despite Campaign A receiving more clicks.

That is a genuine messy-data reasoning problem.

**Priority: STRONG CONCEPTUAL FIT if we make the agent actually take actions/recommend decisions**

## What NOT to build in 36 hours

Avoid trying to implement:

* full Facebook Ads publishing
* full Google Ads publishing
* automatic video generation pipeline
* sophisticated attribution modeling
* production-ready OAuth for every platform
* realtime learning from live advertising campaigns

Instead, demonstrate the **intelligence loop** really well:

> Here is the merchant's store.

> Here are their historical ads and reviews.

> Our system identifies what customers care about.

> It discovers which messaging actually converts.

> And now it automatically builds the merchant's next campaign around that evidence.

That is enough to communicate a much larger product vision while still being realistic for the hackathon.
